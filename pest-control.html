Pest Control Strategies

Protect crops through prevention, monitoring and responsible pest management.

A SMARTER APPROACH

Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

IPM combines different pest management techniques to reduce crop damage, protect beneficial organisms and minimize unnecessary pesticide use.

Farmers identify the pest, monitor its population, assess crop damage and choose appropriate controls based on the crop and local conditions.

FOUR KEY APPROACHES

How to Manage Crop Pests

🛡️

Prevention

Use healthy planting materials, crop rotation, suitable spacing, field sanitation and resistant varieties where available.

🐞

Biological Control

Conserve natural enemies such as ladybirds and parasitoid wasps. Use approved biological control products where suitable.

🌿

Organic Methods

Use suitable physical barriers, hand removal, traps and locally approved botanical products when appropriate.

🔍

Regular Monitoring

Inspect leaves, stems, roots and fruits. Record pest levels, damage and beneficial insects regularly.

Step-by-Step Pest Monitoring

01

Inspect the Field

Walk through different parts of the field and inspect a consistent sample of plants for eggs, insects, disease symptoms and damage.

02

Identify the Problem

Distinguish pest damage from disease, nutrient deficiency, water stress and other causes. Photograph and record symptoms.

03

Assess the Risk

Check pest numbers, crop growth stage, damage level and locally recommended action thresholds.

04

Choose and Review Treatment

Use the least disruptive effective method first. Monitor results and consult an extension officer when the problem persists.

Common Crop Problems

Crop Possible Problem Recommended First Steps
Rice Stem borers Inspect for dead hearts and damaged stems; seek local guidance on monitoring and thresholds.
Tomato Whiteflies Inspect leaf undersides, manage weeds and use suitable physical or biological controls.
Cabbage Caterpillars Check leaves and growing points; remove caterpillars where practical and protect natural enemies.
Mango Fruit flies Collect fallen fruit, maintain orchard sanitation and use recommended monitoring traps.
Important safety advice: Correctly identify the problem before treating it. If a pesticide is necessary, use a locally registered product for the specific crop and pest, follow its label, wear the required protective equipment and observe re-entry and pre-harvest intervals. Never mix household chemicals or unverified homemade pesticide recipes.
