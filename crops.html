🌾 Grains

Rice

A staple food crop widely cultivated in Bangladesh.

Varieties: BRRI dhan28, BRRI dhan29 and other locally suitable varieties.

Soil: Depends on variety; fertile soils with suitable water retention are commonly used.

Cultivation: Direct seeding or transplanting, with appropriate spacing, nutrient and water management.

Harvesting: Harvest when grains reach the recommended maturity and moisture level.

Nutrition: Mainly carbohydrates; provides some protein and B vitamins, especially in less-refined forms.

Uses: Rice meals, flour, puffed rice and rice-based products.

Wheat

An important cereal used for flour and bakery products.

Varieties: BARI Gom varieties and other varieties recommended for the region.

Soil: Well-drained, fertile loam soils are generally suitable.

Cultivation: Timely sowing, appropriate seed rate, balanced nutrition and irrigation.

Harvesting: Harvest when the crop and grain reach suitable maturity.

Nutrition: Carbohydrates, protein, fibre and B vitamins; whole wheat provides more fibre.

Uses: Flour, bread, noodles, biscuits and animal feed.

🍋 Fruits

Mango

A popular tropical fruit grown in many parts of Bangladesh.

Varieties: Langra, Fazli, Himsagar and Amrapali, depending on location.

Soil: Deep, well-drained soil; avoid prolonged waterlogging.

Cultivation: Plant healthy grafted trees, maintain spacing and manage orchard nutrition.

Harvesting: Harvest according to variety, maturity indicators and intended market.

Nutrition: Vitamin C, provitamin A carotenoids and dietary fibre.

Uses: Fresh fruit, juice, pickles, chutney and dried products.

Banana

A widely consumed fruit suitable for warm growing conditions.

Varieties: Amritasagar, Sabri and other locally adapted varieties.

Soil: Fertile, well-drained soil with adequate moisture.

Cultivation: Use healthy planting material and provide water, nutrients and wind protection.

Harvesting: Harvest bunches at the maturity appropriate for transport and consumption.

Nutrition: Carbohydrates, potassium, vitamin B6 and some fibre.

Uses: Fresh fruit, cooked dishes, chips and processed foods.

🥬 Vegetables

Tomato

A versatile vegetable crop grown in suitable seasons.

Varieties: BARI Tomato varieties and locally adapted hybrids.

Soil: Fertile, well-drained loam soil with suitable pH.

Cultivation: Raise healthy seedlings, transplant carefully and support plants when needed.

Harvesting: Pick at the maturity stage required for fresh use or transport.

Nutrition: Vitamin C, potassium and lycopene.

Uses: Salads, sauces, cooking and food processing.

Cabbage

A leafy vegetable commonly grown in cooler conditions.

Varieties: Green cabbage varieties suited to local climate and season.

Soil: Fertile, moist but well-drained soil.

Cultivation: Transplant healthy seedlings, manage nutrients and monitor caterpillars.

Harvesting: Harvest when heads are firm and sufficiently developed.

Nutrition: Vitamin C, vitamin K, folate and fibre.

Uses: Salads, curries, stir-fries and pickles.

🌿 Cash Crops

Jute

An important fibre crop with a long history in Bangladesh.

Varieties: BJRI Tossa and Corchorus capsularis varieties suitable for the area.

Soil: Fertile alluvial soil with adequate moisture and suitable drainage.

Cultivation: Sow quality seed, control weeds and maintain adequate soil moisture.

Harvesting: Cut at the recommended stage and process fibre through retting using appropriate methods.

Nutrition: Grown mainly for fibre, not as a staple food crop.

Uses: Sacks, ropes, textiles, packaging and biodegradable products.

Sugarcane

A tall perennial grass grown for sugar and other products.

Varieties: BSRl-recommended or other locally approved sugarcane varieties.

Soil: Deep, fertile, well-drained soil with adequate moisture.

Cultivation: Plant healthy setts, manage weeds, nutrients and irrigation.

Harvesting: Harvest when the crop reaches maturity for processing.

Nutrition: Mainly valued for sucrose extracted from its stalks.

Uses: Sugar, molasses, juice, ethanol and bagasse for industrial uses.

Crop recommendations vary by region, variety, soil and climate. Confirm local guidance before making cultivation decisions. Nutrition descriptions are general and not laboratory analyses.
